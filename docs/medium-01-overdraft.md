# The $35 fee for having $4 in your account

*Subtitle: Nobody explained overdraft to me. Here's the explanation I should have gotten, and the first stop on a project called Routes.*

I've been investing and trading my own money for years now. I know the fine print. But I remember what it felt like before I did, and I still think it's wrong that the only way most people learn this stuff is by getting charged for it first.

That third part, how it makes money off you, is the whole reason this exists. Every financial product has an answer to that question. Most money content skips it, because the people writing it get paid when you sign up. I don't, so I can say it.

First stop: overdraft. Almost everyone has paid one. Almost nobody has read about it.

## What it is

You spend more than you have in checking. The bank does one of two things. It declines the card, or it covers the purchase and charges you for doing it. That charge has historically been around $30 to $35. A lot of banks have cut it or dropped it in the last few years, but plenty haven't.

## Who ends up using it

Nobody on purpose. It hits people living paycheck to paycheck the hardest, which is exactly why regulators keep circling it.

## How it makes money off you

The fee is the product. For years overdraft fees were one of the biggest sources of fee income at consumer banks. That's not a side effect of the service. That's the service.

Here's the part that matters. Overdraft "protection" is something you opt into. If you never did, your card just declines when you're short. That's usually the better outcome, even though it feels worse in the moment. The bank will frame opting in as a favor to you. Think about who the favor is for.

## What goes wrong

Several fees in one day from small purchases. A coffee, a parking app, a sandwich. Each one over the line, each one $35. Some banks have processed the day's transactions from largest to smallest, which empties your account faster and triggers more fees. It's been litigated. It still happens.

That's how a $4 coffee costs $39.

## What to ask your bank

Am I opted in to overdraft coverage, or will my card decline?

What's the fee, and is there a cap per day?

Can I link my savings account as a backstop instead?

Three questions. Five minutes on the phone. Most people never ask.
