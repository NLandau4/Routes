## What it is

### Short

Selling an investment that's down to realize the loss on paper, then using that loss to offset gains elsewhere, which cuts the tax bill. Losses beyond your gains can offset up to $3,000 of regular income a year, with the rest carrying forward. The catch is the wash-sale rule: buy the same or a 'substantially identical' investment within 30 days before or after the sale and the loss is disallowed.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

People with taxable brokerage accounts and gains to offset. Robo-advisors sell automated versions as a premium feature. Useless inside a 401(k) or IRA, where gains aren't taxed anyway.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The tax benefit is real but it's a deferral, not a gift: the replacement investment has a lower cost basis, so the gain shows up later. Robo-advisors and advisors market it as a return-enhancer worth their fee. The IRS collects when the wash-sale rule is broken by accident.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Rebuying the same fund inside the window, including automatically through dividend reinvestment or a 401(k) contribution to the same fund, which triggers the rule across accounts. Selling a position just for the loss and missing a rebound while sitting in cash. Doing it in a year with no gains and a low bracket, where the loss is worth little.

## What to ask

### Short

What gains do I have this year to offset? What will I hold instead, and is it 'substantially identical'? Do any of my other accounts buy this same fund automatically? What's the new cost basis, and when will that gain come due?
