## What it is

### Short

The tax on the profit when you sell something for more than you paid: stocks, funds, crypto, property. Held a year or less, the gain is 'short-term' and taxed like wages. Held more than a year, it's 'long-term' and taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income. For 2026 the 0% rate covers taxable income up to $49,450 single or $98,900 married filing jointly. Losses offset gains. None of this applies inside a 401(k), IRA, or HSA.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone who sells an investment in a taxable account. Also people selling a home (there's a large exclusion for a primary residence), a business, or collectibles (which have their own rate).

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The government is the one collecting, but the cost shows up where you don't expect: mutual funds that pass capital gains to holders every year whether or not you sold; frequent trading that turns long-term gains into short-term ones; tax software that upsells for investment forms. Brokers report your cost basis, and mistakes there are yours to fix.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Selling at eleven months when another month would have cut the rate. Forgetting that reinvested dividends raise your cost basis, and paying tax twice. Not knowing a fund's distributions are taxable in a taxable account. Trading crypto between coins and not realizing each swap is a sale.

## What to ask

### Short

How long have I held this? What's my cost basis, and does the broker have it right? What bracket does this gain land in? Would this be taxed differently inside a retirement account? Do I have losses that could offset it?
