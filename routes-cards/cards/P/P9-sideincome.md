## What it is

### Short

Money earned outside a regular paycheck: freelancing, gig apps, a small business, selling things. It arrives with no tax withheld, and you owe both income tax and self-employment tax (15.3%, which is both halves of Social Security and Medicare that an employer would otherwise split with you). The IRS expects it paid through the year in four estimated payments, due around April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone with a 1099 instead of a W-2: drivers, contractors, consultants, creators, resellers. Also W-2 employees with a side project that crosses a few hundred dollars of profit.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The IRS charges a penalty for underpaying through the year, even if you pay in full in April. Platforms take their cut before you see the money and then report your gross to the IRS. Tax software charges extra for the self-employment forms. Bookkeeping and 'business formation' services sell structure most small side incomes don't need.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Spending all of it and meeting the tax bill in April. Not tracking expenses, which are deductible and reduce both taxes. Missing the quarterly dates. Not knowing that half the self-employment tax is itself deductible. Assuming a loss-making hobby can offset a salary.

### Short
