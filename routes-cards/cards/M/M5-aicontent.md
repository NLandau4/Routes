## What it is

### Short

Articles, videos, and posts about money produced wholly or mostly by AI, which now make up a large share of what a search returns. They read smoothly, cite nothing, and state figures that are sometimes years stale or invented. The tells: no named author, no date, numbers with no source, generic advice that could apply to anyone, and a product link at the end.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone who searches a money question. The content is built to rank, not to be right, and it increasingly outranks the primary sources it paraphrases.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Ad revenue and affiliate links. A page that explains high-yield savings accounts and ends with three 'top picks' is paid when you click. The explanation is the bait; the links are the business. Volume is cheap, so the incentive is to produce more, not better.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Acting on a rate, limit, or rule that changed two years ago. Trusting a confident paragraph about tax law that was never checked. Not noticing the same wrong figure repeated across ten sites because they all copied each other. Reading an ad as an explainer.

## What to ask

### Short

Who wrote this and when? Where does each number come from, and can I click through to it? Does the page get paid if I click something? Does it tell me what a thing is, or push me toward one? Would this still be true if the writer had nothing to sell me?
