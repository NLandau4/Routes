## What it is

### Short

Someone getting you to send money or information by pretending to be something they're not. People reported about $16 billion in fraud losses in 2025, a record, with $3.5 billion of that from imposters posing as a bank, a government agency, a company, or a relative. The method changes monthly; the structure doesn't.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Everyone, and increasingly younger adults, who report losing money more often than older ones. Nobody thinks they're the target until they are.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The scam is the product; the whole thing is how it makes money off you. The three parts: a promise or a threat (guaranteed returns, your account is compromised, a relative in trouble), urgency (act now, don't tell anyone), and an untraceable payment (wire, crypto, gift cards, a payment app to a stranger).

## What goes wrong

### Short

Acting before checking. Trusting caller ID, an email address, or a voice (all can be faked). Sending a second payment to recover the first, which is its own scam. Not reporting it because of embarrassment.

## What to ask

### Short

Can I hang up and call the organization back at the number on my card or statement? Why does this need to happen right now? Why this payment method? Would a real bank, agency, or employer ever ask for this?
