## What it is

### Short

An automated investing service: answer a questionnaire, get a portfolio of low-cost index funds matched to it, and the service rebalances and (on some tiers) tax-loss harvests for you. Fees are typically around a quarter of a percent a year on top of the funds' own expense ratios. The main examples are standalone apps and the robo arms of the big brokerages.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

People who want to invest without choosing funds and without an advisor's price tag. Common for a first brokerage account or a rollover IRA.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The advisory fee, every year, on the whole balance, regardless of performance. Some keep a cash allocation that earns the firm interest. Premium tiers add human advisors and higher fees. The underlying funds charge their own expense ratios on top.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Paying 0.25% a year for what a single target-date or balanced fund does for less. Multiple accounts across multiple robos, each with its own fee. Not realizing the questionnaire's 'risk score' drives everything and you answered it in five minutes. Assuming the tax-loss harvesting feature is worth the fee when the account is small or in an IRA.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the total I pay, the advisory fee plus the funds' expense ratios? What does a target-date fund with the same mix cost? How much cash does the portfolio hold, and who earns the interest on it? What am I getting that a single fund doesn't do?
