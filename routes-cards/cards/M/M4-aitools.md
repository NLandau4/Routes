## What it is

### Short

Chatbots and apps that answer money questions, categorize spending, or suggest what to do with cash. Some are general assistants; some are built into banks, brokers, and budgeting apps. Four questions sort them: who built it and what they sell, whether it personalizes (and if so, whether anyone is licensed to), what data it keeps, and what it can't know about you.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Nearly everyone now, often without noticing: the 'ask us anything' box in a banking app, the budgeting assistant, the chatbot a friend screenshots. People use them for exactly the questions they're embarrassed to ask a person.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

If it's inside a product, the product's incentives are its incentives: a bank's assistant explains the bank's accounts, a broker's assistant is comfortable with trading. Standalone tools earn through subscriptions, data, or referrals to the things they mention. An answer that ends with a specific product to open is a sales channel, whatever it calls itself.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Trusting a number it stated with confidence and never checking it. Giving it account details it stores. Taking a recommendation from something that isn't licensed to give one and has no liability if it's wrong. Mistaking fluency for accuracy.

## What to ask

### Short

Who made this and how do they make money? Is it telling me what something is, or what I should do? Where did that number come from, and can I find the source? What did I just give it, and where does that go?
