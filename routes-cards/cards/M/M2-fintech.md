## What it is

### Short

Apps that offer checking-like accounts, cards, and savings but aren't banks themselves. They hold your money at a partner bank behind the scenes, often through a middleman that keeps the ledger of whose money is whose. The FDIC insures the bank; it does not insure the app or the middleman. If the app or its ledger-keeper fails, getting your money back depends on how good their records were.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Younger customers, people frustrated with bank fees, gig workers who get paid through the app, and anyone who signed up for a high promotional rate. Many don't know they aren't at a bank.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Interchange fees every time you swipe the card, interest on your deposits that the partner bank shares with the app, subscription tiers, instant-transfer fees, and sometimes the sale of your transaction data. The 'no fees' pitch means no monthly fee; it doesn't mean free.

## What goes wrong

### Short

The middleman failing. In 2024 a ledger company between several apps and their banks collapsed, and customers were locked out for months while regulators tried to reconstruct who was owed what. Thinking 'FDIC insured' on the app's website means the app is insured. No branch and a chatbot when something goes wrong. Account freezes with no human to call.

## What to ask

### Short

Which bank actually holds my money? Is the account in my name at that bank, or in the app's name? What happens to my money if the app shuts down? How do I reach a person?
