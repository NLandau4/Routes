## What it is

### Short

What happens to your accounts and property when you die. A will covers things that don't already have a named recipient. Retirement accounts, life insurance, and many bank and brokerage accounts pass by beneficiary designation, the form on file with the institution, and that form overrides the will. Property with no clear path goes through probate, a court process that is public, slow, and costs money.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Everyone with an account or a dependent, which is everyone. Especially anyone who has married, divorced, or had a child since they opened an account; the beneficiary form doesn't update itself.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Probate fees, attorney fees, and in some states a percentage of the estate. Institutions charge nothing for a beneficiary form, which is why it's the single highest-value piece of paperwork in personal finance. Estate-planning packages and trusts are sold to people whose situation a form and a simple will would cover.

## What goes wrong

### Short

An ex-spouse still named on the 401(k). No beneficiary at all, which sends a retirement account through probate and can trigger a faster, larger tax bill for heirs. A minor named directly, who can't legally receive it. Nobody knowing where the accounts are.

## What to ask

### Short

Who is the beneficiary on each of my accounts, right now? Is there a contingent? Does anyone I trust know what accounts exist? What in my situation actually needs a will or a trust, and what's already handled by the forms?
