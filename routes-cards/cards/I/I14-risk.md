## What it is

### Short

How much of a drop you can sit through without selling. It's a behavior, discovered in a real decline, not a score from a ten-question quiz. It has two parts people conflate: capacity (how much loss your life can absorb: time until you need the money, stability of income) and tolerance (how much you can stomach). A twenty-five-year-old has high capacity and may still have low tolerance.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Every investor, whether they've named it or not. Brokerages and advisors ask about it on sign-up because regulators require them to match what they sell to what the client can bear.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The questionnaire is free. What's sold on the back of it isn't: 'conservative' portfolios heavy in high-fee products, annuities pitched to anyone who checked 'I worry about losses,' and advisory fees for managing anxiety rather than money. Panic-selling in a crash is the most expensive thing most people ever do, and someone is on the other side of that trade.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Answering the questionnaire in a calm year and finding out the answer in a bad one. Confusing a long time horizon with a strong stomach. Holding all stocks because that's what 'aggressive' people do, then selling at the bottom. Holding all cash because a drop once scared you and losing decades to inflation.

## What to ask

### Short

How much did a portfolio like this fall in 2008 and in 2020, in dollars, not percent? What would I have done? When do I need this money? If it dropped by a third tomorrow, what's my plan, written down now?
