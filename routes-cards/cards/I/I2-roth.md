## What it is

### Short

Two tax treatments that apply to retirement accounts (both 401(k)s and IRAs). Traditional: money goes in before tax, grows untaxed, and you pay income tax when you take it out. Roth: money goes in after tax, grows untaxed, and comes out tax-free. Same account, different timing of the tax. An IRA is the version you open yourself, not through work, with lower annual limits.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone saving for retirement in a tax-advantaged account has to pick one, and the choice turns on tax rates now versus later, which nobody knows for sure.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The accounts themselves don't. Brokerages earn on what's inside them and on cash sitting uninvested. Some providers charge IRA account fees; many charge none.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Thinking it's a product rather than a tax label. Contributing to a Roth IRA when income is above the limit. Treating "tax-free later" as free: Roth contributions cost you the deduction today.

## What to ask

### Short

Is my income likely higher or lower now than it will be in retirement? What are this year's contribution limits? For 2026 the IRA limit is $7,500 under age 50, and Roth IRA eligibility phases out above $153,000 of income for single filers and $242,000 for joint filers; they change yearly, so check the IRS page. Does my 401(k) offer both?
