## What it is

### Short

Using borrowed money to control more of an investment than your cash alone would buy. Margin loans at a broker, leveraged ETFs that aim for two or three times an index's daily move, options and futures, and a mortgage on a rental property are all forms. Gains and losses both scale with the borrowed amount; the loan and its interest don't shrink when the asset does.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Traders, by design. Everyday investors, often by accident: a leveraged ETF bought because it was 'the 3x version,' or a margin loan taken to avoid selling. Real estate investors use it deliberately, with the property as collateral.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Interest on the borrowed amount, charged daily, in good markets and bad. Leveraged ETFs carry higher expense ratios and lose value over time from the daily reset even when the index goes nowhere. Brokers collect margin interest and can force-sell your positions at the worst moment to protect their loan.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Losing more than you put in. A margin call that sells you out at the bottom. Holding a daily-reset leveraged fund for months and getting a return nothing like three times the index. Treating leverage as a way to catch up after a loss.

## What to ask

### Short

What am I paying to borrow, and what return does the investment need to cover it? What's the worst case, in dollars? Who decides when I'm sold out, and at what level? Is this fund designed to be held for one day or one year?
