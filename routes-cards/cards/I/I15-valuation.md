## What it is

### Short

The numbers on a stock quote page and what they mean. Market cap: share price times shares outstanding, the total price of the company. P/E ratio: price divided by a year's earnings per share, roughly how many years of profit you're paying for. Earnings per share, revenue, dividend yield, and 52-week range fill out the rest. None is a verdict; each is a measurement.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone who looks at an individual stock. Analysts and fund managers run deeper versions. Most index-fund investors never need them, and that's fine; the numbers matter for understanding what you own, not for timing anything.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The data is free. What's sold is interpretation: stock-picking newsletters, screeners with premium tiers, 'undervalued stock' lists, and courses that teach a formula as if it were a secret. Brokers benefit when the numbers prompt trading.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Comparing P/E across industries where normal ranges differ wildly. Treating a low P/E as 'cheap' when it may mean the market expects earnings to fall. Looking at share price alone, which says nothing without the share count. Mistaking a story about a company for its numbers, or the numbers for the whole story.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the market cap, and what business is actually worth that much? What's the P/E, and what's typical for this industry? Are earnings growing or shrinking? What would have to be true for this price to make sense?
