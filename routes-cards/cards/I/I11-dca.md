## What it is

### Short

Investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule regardless of what the market is doing. When prices are low the amount buys more shares; when high, fewer. Anyone contributing from each paycheck into a 401(k) is already doing it. The alternative, for a lump sum, is investing it all at once.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Most people, by default, because money arrives in paychecks. Also people who received a lump sum (bonus, inheritance, sale) and are nervous about putting it all in the day before a drop.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Nobody charges for the idea. Brokers do earn more when it's done in small frequent trades with spreads or commissions on each one, and apps built around 'round-ups' and micro-investing often charge flat monthly fees that are a large percentage of a small balance.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Treating it as a way to beat the market. Vanguard's research found that for a lump sum, investing it all at once came out ahead of spreading it over three months about two-thirds of the time, because markets have risen more often than fallen. Cost averaging reduces regret, not expected return. Stopping the schedule during a drop, which is the moment it does the most.

## What to ask

### Short

Am I doing this because money arrives monthly, or because I'm nervous about a lump sum? What does each purchase cost me in fees or spread? If the market fell by a third next month, would I keep the schedule?
