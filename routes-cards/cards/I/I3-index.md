## What it is

### Short

A fund that buys a whole list of investments at once, matching an index like the S&P 500 (500 large U.S. companies) rather than trying to pick winners. One purchase gives you a small slice of everything on the list. Comes in two wrappers: a mutual fund (priced once a day) or an ETF (trades like a stock during the day).

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Most first-time investors and a large share of professionals. It's the default inside many 401(k)s and robo-advisors.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The expense ratio, which is small by design, often 0.03 to 0.20% per year. That's the whole pitch: it's cheap because no one is being paid to pick stocks. Some brokers also earn on the cash in your account or on "payment for order flow" when you trade.

## What goes wrong

### Short

It goes down when the market goes down, fully. There's no manager stepping in. Historically broad markets have recovered, but over years, not weeks, and past results don't guarantee future ones. Also: not all "index funds" track broad indexes; some track narrow or gimmicky ones with higher fees.

## What to ask

### Short

What index does it track? What's the expense ratio? Is there a sales charge ("load") or a minimum? Is this a mutual fund or an ETF, and does that matter for how I'd buy it?
