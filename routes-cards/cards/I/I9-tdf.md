## What it is

### Short

A single fund that holds a mix of stocks and bonds and shifts toward bonds as a chosen year (2055, 2060) approaches. You pick the year closest to when you'd retire and it rebalances itself. It's the default option in many 401(k) plans and held $4.8 trillion in 2025.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

People who want one choice and never want to think about it again, and everyone who was auto-enrolled in a 401(k) and never changed the default.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The expense ratio, which averaged 0.27% in 2025 but ranges from under 0.1% to over 0.7% depending on the provider. The fund of funds structure can stack a fee on top of the underlying funds' fees. Compare that to 0.14% for a plain index ETF.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Assuming all target-date funds are cheap. Two funds with the same year can hold very different mixes. Holding one in a brokerage account alongside other funds and ending up with the same thing twice.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the total expense ratio, including the underlying funds? What's the stock/bond mix today and at the target year? Is there a cheaper target-date series in the same plan? Is it built from index funds or actively managed ones?
