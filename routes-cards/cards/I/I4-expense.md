## What it is

### Short

The yearly fee a fund charges, as a percentage of what you have in it, taken out automatically so you never see a bill. 0.05% means $5 a year on $10,000. 1.00% means $100. It's the single most reliable predictor of how one fund will do against a similar one, because it's the one thing known in advance.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone holding a fund, whether they know it or not. It's on every fund's fact sheet and in your 401(k) plan documents.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

This is the fee. It's how fund companies, and often the advisor or plan that put you in the fund, get paid. Because it's a percentage, it grows with your balance for the rest of your life.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Compounding against you. Two funds holding similar things at 0.05% and 1.00% can end up tens of thousands of dollars apart over a working life, on the same contributions. Target-date funds and "managed" options in 401(k)s are where high ratios hide most often.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the expense ratio of each fund I'm in? Is there a similar fund in the menu that's cheaper? Are there other fees on top (advisory, administrative, 12b-1)?
