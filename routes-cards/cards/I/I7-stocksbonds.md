## What it is

### Short

A stock is a slice of ownership in a company: you get a share of its profits if it does well and nothing promised if it doesn't. A bond is a loan to a company or government: they pay you a set interest rate and give the money back at the end. Stocks swing more and have historically returned more; bonds swing less and pay less.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Nearly every investment account holds some of each, usually through funds. The mix is what people mean by how risky a portfolio is.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Stocks: the company earns on your money, not off you, but the fund or broker you hold it through charges fees. Bonds: the borrower pays you interest; the middlemen earn on the spread and on fund fees.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Thinking bond prices only go one way (they fall when rates rise). Holding all stocks and selling in the first drop. Holding all bonds for decades and losing ground to inflation.

### Short
