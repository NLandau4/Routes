## What it is

### Short

A 401(k) is a retirement account offered through an employer. Money goes in from your paycheck before it reaches you. A match is when the employer adds its own money on top, usually a formula like "50% of what you put in, up to 6% of your pay" or "100% of the first 3%, then 50% of the next 2%." The match is part of your pay that you only get if you contribute.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Employees whose company offers one. Not every job does, and some require a waiting period before the match kicks in.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The plan provider charges fees, often buried in the funds inside the plan (see Expense ratio). Some plans have good, cheap fund menus; some have bad, expensive ones, and you can't shop around because your employer picked it.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Leaving the match on the table. Not knowing the vesting schedule: the employer's contributions may not fully belong to you until you've stayed a certain number of years. Early withdrawals before 59½ generally mean taxes plus a 10% penalty.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the match formula, exactly? When am I fully vested? What funds are in the plan and what do they cost? Is there a Roth option inside it?
