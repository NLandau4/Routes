## What it is

### Short

How much a retirement portfolio can pay out each year without running dry. In 1994 a financial planner named William Bengen tested every 30-year stretch since 1926 and found that a first-year withdrawal of 4% of the portfolio, raised each year for inflation, had survived all of them, including the stretch beginning in the late 1960s. That's the origin of 'the 4% rule.' It's a historical finding about a 50/50 stock-bond mix, not a law.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone planning to live off savings, and anyone trying to figure out how much savings that takes. Advisors, planning software, and retirement calculators all build on some version of it.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The research is public. The industry around it sells certainty: annuities marketed as the answer to running out, advisory fees for a withdrawal plan, and 'income' products with high costs. Guaranteed-income pitches target exactly the fear this topic raises.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Treating 4% as safe for any portfolio, any time horizon, any market. A bad first few years (sequence risk) hurts far more than the same bad years later. Withdrawing a fixed amount from an all-stock portfolio. Ignoring taxes: a withdrawal from a traditional IRA is income; from a Roth it isn't.

## What to ask

### Short

What mix is this portfolio, and what time horizon am I planning for? What happens to the plan if the first three years are bad? Which accounts do I draw from first, and what's the tax on each? What's my plan to adjust if the balance falls?
