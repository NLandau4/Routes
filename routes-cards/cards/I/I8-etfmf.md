## What it is

### Short

Two ways to package the same basket of investments. A mutual fund is bought and sold once a day at that day's closing price, often with a minimum investment. An ETF trades on an exchange all day like a stock, with no minimum beyond one share. Many index funds exist in both forms with the same holdings.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone buying a fund. In a 401(k) you usually get mutual funds; in a brokerage account most people buy ETFs.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The expense ratio either way. Mutual funds sometimes add sales charges (loads) or 12b-1 marketing fees. ETFs cost a bid-ask spread each time you trade. In a taxable account ETFs usually pass on fewer capital gains, which matters at tax time.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Paying a load on a mutual fund when a near-identical ETF has none. Trading an ETF often because you can. Buying an ETF at a thin moment and paying a wide spread.

## What to ask

### Short

Is there a sales charge or a minimum? What's the expense ratio for each version? Does this fund distribute capital gains every year? If I'm in a 401(k), which wrapper does the plan actually offer?
