## What it is

### Short

A three-digit number, usually 300 to 850, that lenders use to guess how likely you are to pay back what you borrow. Built from your history: whether you pay on time, how much of your available credit you use, how long you've had accounts, and how often you apply for new ones. The most common versions are FICO and VantageScore, and you have several scores, not one.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone who will ever rent an apartment, get a phone plan, finance a car, or take a mortgage. Employers and insurers sometimes check it too.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) sell your data to lenders. "Free credit score" apps are usually paid by the cards and loans they show you next to your score. Paid "credit monitoring" is often selling what you can get free by law.

## What goes wrong

### Short

A low score makes every loan cost more, sometimes for years. Errors on your report are common and are yours to dispute. Checking your own score does not hurt it; a lender's "hard pull" when you apply does, a little.

### Short

Which score is this (FICO? Vantage? which version)? What's dragging it down: late payments, high utilization, short history? When did I last pull my free reports from the three bureaus?
