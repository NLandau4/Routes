## What it is

### Short

The arithmetic of getting out of debt. Two numbers decide it: the rate and how much more than the minimum you pay. At a 22% card rate, paying only the minimum on a balance can take well over a decade and cost more in interest than the original purchases. Every card statement is required to show you this calculation.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone carrying a balance on more than one thing: cards, a BNPL plan, a car, a student loan. The question is which to pay down first.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Lenders earn on the balance, so the longer it lasts the more they make. The minimum payment is set low enough to keep the balance alive for years. Debt-relief companies charge fees for plans you can usually set up yourself.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Paying extra on the lowest-rate loan first. Consolidating into a new loan and then running the cards back up. Not noticing a 0% promo ending and interest charging back from day one.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the rate on each balance? What's the minimum on each, and what happens if I put the extra on the highest rate first versus the smallest balance first? How long does the statement say this balance takes at the minimum?
