## What it is

### Short

Annual percentage rate: the yearly cost of borrowing, shown as a percentage. It's the borrowing twin of APY. The average rate on cards that carried a balance was about 22% in August 2026. At 24%, a $1,000 balance carried for a year costs roughly $240 in interest, charged a bit each day. Loans with fees fold those fees into APR, which is why APR is the number to compare across lenders.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Anyone with a card, a car loan, a mortgage, a student loan, or a BNPL plan. It's on every one of them, by law.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

It is how they make money. The trick is where it's shown: big for the 0% promo, small for what comes after. "Variable" APR means it moves when the Federal Reserve moves rates, which is most cards.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Not knowing yours. Promotional 0% periods that end and sometimes charge back interest from day one ("deferred interest") if any balance remains.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the APR after any promo? Fixed or variable? Is this "0% APR" or "no interest if paid in full," which is a different and worse thing?
