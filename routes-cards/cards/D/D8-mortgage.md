## What it is

### Short

A loan to buy a home, secured by the home. Most are fixed for 15 or 30 years; in late October 2026 the average 30-year rate was 7.32% and the 15-year 6.68%. Put down less than 20% on a conventional loan and the lender adds private mortgage insurance (PMI), a monthly charge that protects the lender, not you. Escrow is the extra each month the lender collects to pay your property tax and homeowner's insurance.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Most homebuyers. First-time buyers are the ones most likely to pay PMI, because 20% of a house price is a lot of cash. FHA and VA loans have their own versions of mortgage insurance with different rules.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Interest over decades: on a 30-year loan the first years' payments are mostly interest. Then origination fees, points, and closing costs at the start. PMI is pure margin for the lender's risk. Servicers also earn float on the escrow balance they hold.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Shopping by monthly payment and ignoring the rate and fees behind it. Not knowing PMI can be removed: by law you can ask once the loan hits 80% of the original value, and it drops automatically at 78%. Escrow shortfalls when taxes or insurance rise, which show up as a payment jump. Stretching to the maximum the lender approves.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the APR, including fees, not just the rate? What's the total I'd pay over the life of the loan? When does PMI come off, and do I have to ask? What's in the escrow estimate, and what happens when it's short?
