## What it is

### Short

A card that lets you borrow from the card company each time you buy something. You get a bill once a month. Pay the full balance by the due date and you pay no interest. Pay less than that and interest starts on what's left, at a rate that's usually among the highest of any common loan.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Most adults. Used in full each month, it's a convenient way to pay that builds credit history and sometimes earns rewards. Used as a loan, it's expensive.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Three ways: interest on balances you carry (the big one), a fee the store pays on every swipe (which funds rewards), and annual or late fees. Rewards programs are designed to work only if you spend more, and they're funded by people who carry balances.

## What goes wrong

### Short

The minimum payment. It's set low enough that a balance can take years and cost more in interest than the original purchase. Also: a card is not an emergency fund, though it gets used as one.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the APR, and is there a different one for cash advances? Is there an annual fee? What's the grace period? If I only paid the minimum, how long would this balance take to clear (the statement has to tell you)?
