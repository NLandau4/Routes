## What it is

### Short

Money borrowed for school. Federal loans come from the government with a fixed rate set each year (6.52% for undergraduates in 2026-27), income-based repayment options, and no credit check. Private loans come from banks, with rates set by your credit and fewer ways out if things go wrong.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

About half of people who finish a four-year degree, and plenty who don't finish. Parents too, through PLUS loans, which carry the highest federal rate.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Interest. On an unsubsidized loan it starts accruing the day the money is sent, while you're still in school, and gets added to the balance when repayment begins. Servicers are paid by the government to collect; private lenders keep the spread between what they borrow at and what they charge you.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Not knowing which type you have, because the rules are different. Interest capitalizing during a deferment. Missing that federal loans have income-based plans and private ones usually don't. Refinancing federal loans into private ones and losing those protections for a slightly lower rate.

## What to ask

### Short

Are these federal or private? Subsidized or not? What's the rate on each loan, and is interest accruing right now? Who is the servicer, and what repayment plans am I eligible for?
