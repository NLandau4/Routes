## What it is

### Short

A loan to buy a vehicle, with the vehicle as collateral: miss payments and it can be repossessed. You pay the price plus interest over a set term. In early 2026 the average new-car loan was about $44,000 at $770 a month over 69 months, and more than a third of new loans ran longer than six years.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Most people who buy a car. Dealers arrange many of these loans on the spot, often through their own lender partners.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Interest, and the term. Stretching a loan from 60 to 84 months lowers the monthly payment and raises the total you pay, which is why the conversation starts with the payment, not the price. Dealers can also earn a markup on the rate they arrange for you, and sell add-ons (warranties, protection plans) rolled into the loan.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Being underwater: owing more than the car is worth for the first few years, which is nearly guaranteed on a long loan with a small down payment. Negotiating the payment instead of the price. Not knowing the rate until the paperwork.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the total price of the car, and what's the APR? What's the term? What would the payment be at a shorter term? Is there a rate from my own bank or credit union to compare against before I sit down?
