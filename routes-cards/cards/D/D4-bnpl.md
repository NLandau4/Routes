## What it is

### Short

Splitting a purchase into a few payments, typically four over six weeks, offered at checkout by companies like Affirm, Klarna, and Afterpay, and increasingly by card issuers themselves. The short "pay in 4" plans usually charge no interest. Longer plans for bigger purchases often do.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Online shoppers, heavily under 35, and increasingly for groceries and everyday items, which is what regulators watch.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The store pays the BNPL company a fee (often 2 to 8% of the sale) for the extra sales it drives. You pay late fees if you miss an installment, and interest on the longer plans. The business model depends on people buying more than they would have with one payment.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Stacking: four or five plans running at once across different apps, with no single bill. Affirm began reporting all its plans to Experian in April 2025, and FICO released score versions that include BNPL data in late 2025, so these plans are starting to show up on credit files. Returns and refunds get messy when the merchant and the lender are different companies.

## What to ask

### Short

Is this plan 0%, and what's the APR if not? What's the late fee? Does this report to credit bureaus, and does paying on time help or only hurt? How many of these do I have open right now?
