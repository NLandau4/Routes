## What it is

### Short

Borrowing from your broker against the investments in your account to buy more. Federal rules let you borrow up to half the purchase price; brokers require you to keep at least 25% equity after that, and most set a higher bar. Fall below it and you get a margin call: add cash or the broker sells your holdings, at its choice of which and when.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Active traders by design. Ordinary investors sometimes by accident, when an app enables margin by default or offers 'instant buying power.' Some use margin loans as a cheap way to borrow against a portfolio without selling.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Interest on the loan, compounded daily, often at rates well above a mortgage. The broker also earns by lending out the shares you bought on margin to short sellers, and from the extra trading margin encourages. A forced sale in a crash is executed to protect the broker's loan, not your position.

## What goes wrong

### Short

A 30% drop in a fully margined account is a 60% drop in your equity, plus a margin call at the bottom. The broker selling the position you'd have kept. Not realizing margin was on. Using it to average down into a falling stock.

## What to ask

### Short

Is margin enabled on this account, and did I ask for it? What's the interest rate? At what price would I get a call, and what happens if I don't answer the phone? What's the worst case in dollars?
