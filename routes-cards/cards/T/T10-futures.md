## What it is

### Short

A contract to buy or sell something at a set price on a future date: grain, oil, gold, a stock index, interest rates. Standardized futures date to Chicago in the 1860s, built so farmers and millers could fix a price ahead of harvest. Today most volume is financial. You post a small deposit (margin) against a much larger contract, so leverage is built in, and gains and losses are settled to your account every day.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Producers and big users hedging real exposure: farmers, airlines, refiners, pension funds. Speculators providing the other side. Retail traders through 'micro' contracts and through ETFs that hold futures (most commodity funds work this way).

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Exchange and broker fees per contract, interest on the margin deposit the broker holds, and the daily spread. Funds that hold futures pay a 'roll' cost each time a contract expires, which can quietly erode returns even when the commodity price is flat.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Leverage that turns a small move into a wipeout and a margin call. Holding a contract into delivery. Buying an oil ETF, watching oil rise, and losing money anyway because of roll costs. Not understanding that losses are debited nightly, not at the end.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the full contract value, not just the margin? What does a one-percent move do to my account? When does this expire and what happens then? If this is a fund, what's the roll cost been over the past year?
