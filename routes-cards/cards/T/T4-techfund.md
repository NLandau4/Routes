## What it is

### Short

Two schools of picking what to buy. Technical analysis studies price and volume patterns on charts, on the theory that past movement predicts future movement. Fundamental analysis studies the business: earnings, debt, growth, competition, on the theory that price eventually follows value. Most professional managers use the second; most retail trading apps are built around the first.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Traders use technicals. Analysts and long-term investors lean fundamental. Most people mix them without naming either. Course sellers and chart-pattern influencers target beginners with the first because it looks learnable in a weekend.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Charting platforms, data subscriptions, trading courses, signal services, and newsletters, most priced monthly. Brokers earn when either method produces trades, and technical analysis produces far more of them.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Seeing patterns in noise. The long-running SPIVA scorecards find most professional stock pickers, with teams and data, trail a plain index fund over 15 years; a retail trader with a pattern and a phone is unlikely to do better. Paying for a course that teaches what's free. Confusing a strategy that worked in a bull market with one that works.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the evidence this method beats an index after costs? Who's selling it to me and what do they earn? How would I know if it stopped working? What's my result over the last two years compared to just holding a broad fund?
