## What it is

### Short

A contract on 100 shares. A call is the right to buy at a set price (the strike) before a set date (expiry); a put is the right to sell. The buyer pays a premium for that right. The seller collects the premium and takes on the obligation. Options lose value as expiry approaches, so a buyer needs to be right about direction, size, and timing; the seller is paid for the buyer's chance of missing one of them.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Professionals, for hedging and income. Retail traders, in large numbers since commissions went to zero, mostly buying short-dated calls on the apps that make it feel like a game. Covered calls (selling calls against stock you own) are the common 'income' version.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The premium flows from buyers to sellers, and sellers as a group have a structural edge because time is on their side. Brokers earn on every contract, and options order flow is far more valuable to market makers than stock order flow, so apps push options hard. Spreads on options are wide, especially on far-out strikes.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Buying short-dated options because they're cheap and watching them go to zero in a week. Selling 'naked' calls with unlimited loss. Getting assigned on a short put and owning stock you didn't budget for. Treating a 10x story as the typical outcome rather than the rare one.

## What to ask

### Short

Am I the buyer or the seller here, and what's my maximum loss? What has to happen, by when, for this to profit? What's the spread on this contract? Does the app earn more when I trade options than stock?
