## What it is

### Short

The difference between the highest price a buyer will pay (the bid) and the lowest a seller will accept (the ask). Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, and you've lost the spread before anything moves. On a heavily traded stock or ETF it's a penny; on a small company, a niche ETF, an option, or a crypto token it can be a percent or more. It's widest at the open, the close, and in a panic.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Everyone who trades, every time. Index investors pay it rarely; day traders pay it constantly, which is a large part of why frequent trading is expensive even with zero commissions.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Market makers earn the spread: they buy at the bid, sell at the ask, and pocket the difference thousands of times a day. Brokers that route your orders to those market makers are paid for sending them. 'Commission-free' moved the cost from a line item into the spread.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Trading a thin ETF because it had the right name and paying a wide spread each way. Placing a market order on an option and filling at the far edge of a wide spread. Not noticing the cost because it never appears as a fee. Day-trading a small account where spreads alone eat the gains.
