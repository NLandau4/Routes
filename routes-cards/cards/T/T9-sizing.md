## What it is

### Short

Deciding how much of the account goes into any one position. The usual framing: how much would I lose if this is wrong, and what fraction of the account is that? Traders often cap the loss per trade at a small fixed fraction of the account so that a string of losses, which happens to everyone, can't end it. The size follows from the distance to the exit, not from conviction.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Every trader, and every investor who's ever put a large share of their money in one stock. The employee with most of their net worth in company shares has a position-sizing problem whether or not they trade.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Nobody charges for the idea; the cost of ignoring it is paid to the market. Brokers earn more from large, frequent, concentrated bets than from small careful ones, and app design (leaderboards, confetti, 'buying power') pushes toward the former.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Betting big on a sure thing. Averaging down into a loser until it's most of the account. Sizing by how confident you feel instead of how much you'd lose. Surviving ten trades and being wiped out by the eleventh. Never writing down the exit before entering.

## What to ask

### Short

If this goes against me, where do I get out, and what's that in dollars? What fraction of the account is that? How many losses in a row could I survive? Is this size based on the math or on how sure I feel?
