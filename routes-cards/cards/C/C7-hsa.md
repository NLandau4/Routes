## What it is

### Short

A savings account tied to a high-deductible health plan. Contributions skip income tax, growth isn't taxed, and withdrawals for medical costs aren't taxed either: three breaks, where a 401(k) or Roth gets two. In 2026 the limits are $4,400 for one person and $8,750 for a family. Unlike a flexible spending account, the balance rolls over forever and goes with you when you change jobs.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

People on a high-deductible plan through work or the marketplace. Some use it as a checking account for copays. Others pay medical bills out of pocket, invest the HSA, and keep the receipts, treating it as a second retirement account that happens to be medical.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The bank or custodian running the account charges a monthly fee, pays little interest on the cash portion, and often requires a minimum cash balance before you can invest the rest. Some charge for the investment menu too. Employers pick the custodian; you can move the money to another one.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Spending it on something that isn't a qualified medical cost, which brings tax plus a penalty before 65. Leaving years of contributions in cash. Not realizing the high-deductible plan itself can cost more if you need a lot of care that year. Losing receipts for the expenses you meant to reimburse later.

## What to ask

### Short

Is my health plan HSA-eligible? What does the custodian charge, and what's the cash minimum before investing? Does my employer contribute anything? If I leave this job, can I move the account?
