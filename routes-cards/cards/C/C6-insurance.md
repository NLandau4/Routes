## What it is

### Short

You pay a premium each month, and if a covered bad thing happens, the insurer pays for it, after you pay the deductible. Health, auto, renters or homeowners, and term life are the ones most people need at some point. Each has a limit on what it pays and a list of what it doesn't cover.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Health: nearly everyone. Auto: required by every state and every lender. Renters: anyone renting, and it's cheap. Term life: people with someone who depends on their income.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

Premiums are priced so that, across all customers, the insurer takes in more than it pays out. Riders, low deductibles, and add-ons raise the premium more than they raise the coverage. Whole life and other investment-style policies carry high commissions, which is why they're sold hard.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Being underinsured on the big things (liability, health) and overinsured on the small ones (phone, extended warranties). Not knowing the deductible until the claim. Letting a policy lapse and finding out the gap isn't covered.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the deductible and what's the maximum it pays? What specifically is excluded? Is this term or permanent life insurance, and why? What does the same coverage cost from two other companies?
