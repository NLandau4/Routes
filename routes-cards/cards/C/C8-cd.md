## What it is

### Short

A certificate of deposit: you hand the bank a sum for a set term (three months to five years) at a rate fixed for the whole term. At the end you get the sum back plus the interest. Take it out early and you pay a penalty, usually a few months of interest. Covered by FDIC insurance like any deposit.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

People with money they know they won't touch for a while and who want a rate locked in. Common for a house down payment a year out, or a chunk of an emergency fund past the first few months. Also popular with anyone who doesn't trust themselves not to dip into savings.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

The bank lends your locked-up money out at a higher rate and keeps the difference, same as any deposit, but a CD lets it plan because it knows the money stays. The early-withdrawal penalty is the product: it's what makes the higher rate possible. Many CDs auto-renew at whatever the rate is that day, which is often lower than the one you signed up for.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Needing the money early and eating the penalty. Locking a long term right before rates go up and watching savings accounts pass you. Missing the renewal window and getting rolled into a worse rate. Chasing a promo rate at a bank you'll never use again.

## What to ask

### Short

What's the penalty, in months of interest? What happens at maturity if I do nothing? Is this rate fixed for the whole term? How does it compare to a plain high-yield savings account right now, and what am I giving up for the difference?
