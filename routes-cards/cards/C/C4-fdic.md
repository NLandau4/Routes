## What it is

### Short

A government guarantee that if your bank fails, you get your money back, up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per account category. Credit unions have the same thing under a different name (NCUA). It covers deposits like checking, savings, and CDs. It does not cover investments.

## Who ends up using it

### Short

Everyone with a bank account, automatically, as long as the bank is a member. Nearly all U.S. banks are.

## How it makes money off you

### Short

It doesn't. Banks pay premiums into the fund. The thing to watch is companies that aren't banks (some fintech apps, some crypto platforms) using the FDIC logo loosely. Their coverage, if any, runs through a partner bank and can have gaps.

## What goes wrong

### Short

Assuming an app is a bank. Keeping more than $250,000 at one bank in one name. Confusing "FDIC insured" (deposits) with "SIPC" (brokerage accounts, which protect against the broker failing, not against investments losing value).

_Not yet written._

## What to ask

### Short

Is this company itself an FDIC-insured bank, or does it hold my money at a partner bank? Which partner? Does coverage apply if the app, not the bank, goes under?
